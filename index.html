What’s New

  • Nov. 2026

    I helped create and release Maneleon at the 3rd Scientific Game Jam Tokyo, working as a researcher and programmer. You play as a chameleon that has to blend in by matching patterns to fool AI and matching shapes to fool animals. The game turns a question from vision research into a rule you can play with. [Game]

  • Oct. 2026

    My first-author preprint, Subspace Image Synthesis for Style-Invariant Object Recognition, is now available on bioRxiv. We compared two sets of features in neural networks and used them to generate images for a human experiment. Participants more often chose images generated from features that stayed stable across style changes as the better match to a reference style. [Preprint] [Code]

  • Aug. 2026

    I presented Downsampled Representations Improve Cross-Model Transfer in hV4 Encoding-Model Metamers at CCN 2026 in New York. We generated pairs of images that elicited similar predicted responses in models of the visual area hV4. We then tested whether those matches held in independently trained models and found that they transferred better when we constrained the images to emphasize coarser visual structure. [Abstract]

  • Aug. 2026

    Our paper MAME: Multidimensional Adaptive Metamer Exploration with Human Perceptual Feedback was published in Journal of Vision. We developed a method that uses participants’ responses to adjust images during an experiment. It lets us explore which changes people can detect and which leave images looking the same, across many dimensions in a single experiment. [Paper]

  • Apr. 2026

    Our conference abstract, Investigating the Use of Shape and Texture Information Without Task-Specific Judgment Instructions, was published in VISION. The study asks which cues people rely on when the instructions leave them free to use either shape or texture. [Abstract]

  • Nov. 2025

    I took part in ASCONE 2025, the Japanese Neural Network Society’s Autumn School for Computational Neuroscience. The four-day school combined lectures on computational neuroscience with group exercises and discussions.

  • Sep. 2025

    I presented Investigating the Use of Shape and Texture Information Without Task-Specific Judgment Instructions at the Vision Society of Japan Summer Meeting in Morioka, and received a Best Presentation Award. We investigated how people use shape and texture cues to make visual judgments when the task leaves the choice of cue up to them.

  • Mar. 2025

  • Sep. 2024

    I presented The Role of Visual Feature Detectors That Respond Robustly Across Diverse Object Styles at the Vision Society of Japan Summer Meeting in Tokyo. We investigated which features help a neural network recognize objects when their visual style changes.

  • Jan. 2024

    I presented Robustness of Object Recognition in Pruned Recurrent Neural Networks at the Vision Society of Japan Winter Meeting in Tokyo. We used network pruning to study which parts of a recurrent model contribute to robust object recognition.
